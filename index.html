Render the Robot, Generate the World

One video world model across embodiments

Abstract

Video world models usually learn how a robot moves from raw action vectors. Doppel instead renders the robot along its planned motion, from the camera's viewpoint, and conditions one shared video model on these renders together with the actions. Camera poses missing from robot datasets are recovered from the videos. One model trained on eight datasets (about 670 hours of robot arms, a bimanual robot, a humanoid, and human hands and bodies) improves PSNR over action-only baselines on the three robot-arm datasets by 1.00 dB on unseen trajectories and 1.20 dB out of distribution.

Long horizon

Rollouts from a single frame on held-out test episodes. Each step continues from the model's own last frame, so the model never sees the ground truth again. Errors accumulate over the rollout.

Camera extrinsics

Rendering the robot needs the camera pose. RoboMIND, Bridge and ABC don't provide it, so we recover it from the videos: render the robot at its logged joint angles and optimize the camera until the render lines up with the video.

Method

Doppel conditions Wan 2.1 I2V 14B on render-enriched dense representations, injected as per-token timestep embeddings.

Data

Eight datasets: about 670 hours of video and 803,602 training clips, with one shared action encoder and no embodiment label. Each dataset has 200 validation clips (unseen trajectories) and 200 test clips (the listed shift).

Limitations

Geometry and a camera

Building the renderings needs a URDF, accurate robot geometry and a calibrated camera. Unknown or rapidly changing viewpoints remain a limitation.

Configuration trajectories

Doppel conditions on joint-space trajectories. Imperfect geometry or lower-level control commands would require modeling uncertainty in execution.

Seen embodiments, one camera

Evaluation covers embodiments seen in training and one camera per trajectory. Transfer to unseen embodiments and multi-view prediction are future work.

Long horizons

Long rollouts accumulate error: PSNR falls by about 5 dB from start to end.
