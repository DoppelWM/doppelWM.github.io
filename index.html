Render the Robot, Generate the World

One video world model across embodiments

Abstract

Video world models usually learn how a robot moves from raw action vectors. Doppel renders the robot along its planned joint trajectory from the recorded camera and conditions one shared video model on these renderings together with the actions; camera poses for datasets that lack them are recovered automatically from the videos. Trained jointly on Franka, UR5e and WidowX data, it improves PSNR over per-robot action-only experts by 1.31 dB on unseen trajectories and 1.36 dB out of distribution.

Actions alone, or the rendered robot

The usual way to make a video model action-conditioned is to give it the action vectors: here the UR5e's six joint angles and gripper value at each of 17 frames. Doppel also renders the robot along the same actions, from the recorded camera, and conditions on the rendering and the actions together. Both models start from the same first frame, in a scene whose objects were held out from training.

Camera extrinsics

Rendering the robot from the recorded viewpoint needs the camera's pose relative to the robot base. DROID ships it; RoboMIND and Bridge do not. We recover it from the videos: the robot's 3D Gaussian model is rendered at the logged joint angles, and the camera pose is optimised until the rendering lines up with the recording. No calibration target is used.

Method

Doppel adapts Wan 2.1 I2V 14B, a pretrained image-to-video model. A rendering-enriched action adapter turns the future robot renderings and the joint trajectory into one conditioning vector per video token. The adapter and backbone are shared by every robot. Each robot keeps only its own frozen renderer and action normalization.

Data

Doppel trains jointly on DROID, RoboMIND and Bridge, sampling the three datasets uniformly. Single-robot baselines train on one dataset each. Every dataset has 200 validation clips of unseen trajectories and 200 test clips with a distribution shift.

Results

Doppel, one model for all three robots, beats each robot's action-only expert on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS and SCS, on unseen trajectories and under distribution shift (a new lab for DROID, new objects for RoboMIND, a new kitchen for Bridge). Click a score to see its training curve.

Limitations

Geometry and a camera

Building the renderings needs a URDF, accurate robot geometry and a calibrated camera. Unknown or rapidly changing viewpoints remain a limitation.

Configuration trajectories

Doppel conditions on joint-space trajectories. Imperfect geometry or lower-level control commands would require modeling uncertainty in execution.

Seen embodiments

Evaluation covers robots seen in training and one camera per trajectory. Unseen robots and multi-view prediction are future work.
