Render the Robot, Generate the World

One video world model across embodiments

Abstract

Video world models usually learn how a robot moves from raw action vectors. Doppel renders the body along its planned trajectory from the recorded camera and conditions one shared video model on these renderings together with the actions; camera poses that robot datasets lack are recovered from the videos. One model trained on eight datasets, about 670 hours of video of robot arms, a bimanual robot, a humanoid, and human hands and bodies, with no embodiment label, improves PSNR over action-only baselines on the three robot-arm datasets by 1.00 dB on average on unseen trajectories and 1.20 dB out of distribution. All compared models saw a similar amount of each robot dataset, 3.7 to 5.0M training clips (repeats counted); the eight-dataset model additionally saw 35M clips from the five other datasets.

Actions alone, or the rendered robot

The usual way to make a video model action-conditioned is to give it the action vectors: here the UR5e's six joint angles and gripper value at each of 17 frames. Doppel also renders the robot along the same actions, from the recorded camera, and conditions on the rendering and the actions together. Both models start from the same first frame, in a scene whose objects were held out from training.

Long horizon

25.6 s from a single recorded frame, on held-out test episodes, by Doppel trained on all eight datasets. The rollout chains eight 3.2 s windows: each window starts from the model's own last predicted frame, with that window's render and actions, so after the first frame the model never sees the recording. Errors accumulate; over the held-out test episodes, PSNR falls by about 5 dB from the first window to the last.

Camera extrinsics

Rendering the robot from the recorded viewpoint needs the camera's pose relative to the robot base. DROID, T-Rex and the three human datasets ship it; RoboMIND, Bridge and ABC do not. We recover it from the videos: the robot's 3D Gaussian model is rendered at the logged joint angles, and the camera pose is optimized until the rendering lines up with the recording. No calibration target is used. The loop below is the one used for UR5e and WidowX; ABC (YAM) cameras were fitted per video to SAM 2 robot masks, refined photometrically, and kept only if they passed a held-out IoU check.

Method

Doppel adapts Wan 2.1 I2V 14B, a pretrained image-to-video model. A rendering-enriched action adapter turns the future robot renderings and the joint trajectory into one conditioning vector per video token. The adapter and backbone are shared by every embodiment. Each embodiment keeps only its own frozen renderer and action normalization.

Data

The full model trains on eight datasets, about 670 hours of video and 803,602 training clips, with one shared action encoder and no embodiment label, sampling each dataset in proportion to its training clips. The three-robot model uses DROID, RoboMIND and Bridge, sampled uniformly; single-robot baselines train on one dataset each. Every dataset has 200 validation clips of unseen trajectories and 200 test clips that hold out the listed shift. All evaluated embodiments appear in training.

Results

Final checkpoints. Doppel improves whole-frame prediction over action-only conditioning and fine-tuned OSCAR on all three robot datasets, on unseen trajectories and under distribution shift (a new lab for DROID, a new kitchen for Bridge, new objects for RoboMIND). The rendering carries the gain: render-only conditioning matches render plus actions to within 0.02 dB on unseen trajectories. Training on all eight datasets raises PSNR over the three-robot model on four of the six robot evaluation sets and lowers it by 0.24 dB on Bridge OOD. Limitations

Geometry and a camera

Building the renderings needs a URDF, accurate robot geometry and a calibrated camera. Unknown or rapidly changing viewpoints remain a limitation.

Configuration trajectories

Doppel conditions on joint-space trajectories. Imperfect geometry or lower-level control commands would require modeling uncertainty in execution.

Seen embodiments, one camera

Evaluation covers embodiments seen in training and one camera per trajectory. Transfer to unseen embodiments and multi-view prediction are future work.

Long horizons

Chaining 3.2 s predictions into long rollouts accumulates error: over 25.6 s of held-out test episodes, PSNR falls by about 5 dB from the first window to the last. Late training also improved the seen Bridge kitchens while slightly lowering the unseen one.
